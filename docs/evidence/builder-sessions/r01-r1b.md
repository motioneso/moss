## 2. The 46-minute total (derived)

pg-boss 12.18.2 computes each wait as `LEAST(retry_delay_max, retry_delay * (2^(n+1)/2 + 2^(n+1)/2 * random()))` where n is the retry count so far. That equals `15 * 2^n * (1 + random)`, a random value between `15 * 2^n` and `30 * 2^n`, capped at 900. There are eight waits (n = 0 to 7).

| Wait | n | Minimum (s) | Maximum (s) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0 | 15 | 30 |
| 2 | 1 | 30 | 60 |
| 3 | 2 | 60 | 120 |
| 4 | 3 | 120 | 240 |
| 5 | 4 | 240 | 480 |
| 6 | 5 | 480 | 900 (960 capped) |
| 7 | 6 | 900 (960 capped) | 900 |
| 8 | 7 | 900 (1920 capped) | 900 |
| Total | | 2745 | 3630 |

So 2745 s is arithmetically right, but it is the shortest possible total, not the expected one. The doubling-without-jitter case the text describes is the floor. Real totals fall between 2745 s (45.75 min) and 3630 s (60.5 min), and each of the eight retries also adds up to 2 s of poll delay plus the job's own run time.

Consequence: section 6 sets the injected-failure window to "never shorter than 50 minutes". A delivery whose waits land near the top of the range arrives after about 61 minutes, and would be called unresolved, then deliver after the case closed. That is the exact failure finding 2 was raised to prevent.
