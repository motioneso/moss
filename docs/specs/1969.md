# Spec 1969 — plain-language schedule summary and next-three-doses preview

Re-sliced from #1965 (piece 2 of 4). Builds alongside piece 1; no dependency on it. Piece 3
(the builder form) will call this logic later — wiring it into any screen is out of scope here.

## Problem

Nothing today turns a saved medication schedule into a sentence a person can read, and nothing
works out when the next few doses are due. The builder form (a later piece) needs both so someone
can check what they picked before saving.

## What this delivers

Two pure functions in the wellness package, covering all eight `frequency_type` values stored on
a medication (the six schedule "shapes" from #1950 — daily, selected weekdays, every-N-hours,
cyclical, every-interval, monthly — plus the two flavors of "daily" the UI exposes, and "as
needed"):

1. **`describeSchedule(medication: Medication): string`** — a plain sentence describing when the
   medication is taken. Examples:
   - once a day / N times a day: `"Once a day, at 8:00 AM."` / `"Twice a day, at 8:00 AM and
     8:00 PM."` / `"3 times a day, at 8:00 AM, 2:00 PM, and 8:00 PM."`
   - specific weekdays: `"Every Monday and Thursday, at 9:00 AM."`
   - every N hours: `"Every 6 hours, starting at 8:00 AM."`
   - cyclical: `"2 days on, 3 days off, starting 15 June 2026, at 7:00 AM."`
   - every interval (days/weeks/months): `"Every 2 weeks on Monday, starting 3 March 2026, at
     9:00 AM."`, `"Every 3 days, starting 1 January 2026, at 8:00 AM."`, `"Every 2 months,
     starting 3 March 2026, at 9:00 AM."`
   - monthly (numbered date or weekday position): `"On the 15th of each month, at 8:00 AM."`,
     `"On the last day of each month, at 8:00 AM."`, `"On the first Monday of each month, at
     8:00 AM."`
   - as needed: `"As needed."`
   - an end date, if the medication has one, is appended: `"... until 1 September 2026."`

2. **`nextDoses(medication: Medication, from: Date, count = 3): Date[]`** — the next `count`
   scheduled dose instants at or after `from`. Returns `[]` for an as-needed medication or a
   schedule that produces no occurrences (e.g. a misconfigured one). Always returns UTC instants
   (`Date` objects); the caller formats for display.

Both respect the time zone stored on the medication (`medication.time_zone`, falling back to
`"UTC"` when unset) for every schedule family — this is a deliberate difference from
`computeSchedule` in `packages/wellness/src/schedule.ts`, which pins some families to a UTC-only
naive-civil-time model for its own reasons (documented in that file's module comment). A
plain-language summary and a forward-looking preview are read against real calendar dates, so they
use the stored zone throughout instead.

## Out of scope

- Wiring either function into the builder form or any other screen (piece 3).
- Any change to `computeSchedule`, the day-grid screen, or the occurrence engine's public API.
- Localization/i18n — English sentences only.

## Verification

- Unit tests in `tests/unit/wellness-schedule-summary.test.ts` covering all eight
  `frequency_type` values for both functions, plus: an end date appended to the sentence, a
  non-UTC time zone shifting the printed clock time and the UTC instant, and an as-needed
  medication returning `"As needed."` / `[]`.
- `pnpm --filter @moss/wellness typecheck` and the unit test file green.
- Pure logic with no schema, route, or UI change — no live-UI proof applies. Verified instead by
  running the two functions against a handful of real medication rows on the dev database (via a
  throwaway script, not committed) and confirming the sentences and next-dose times read
  correctly for a real stored medication, including one with a non-UTC time zone.
